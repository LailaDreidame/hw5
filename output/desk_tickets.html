Reflection

My reflection on the Problem 9 run (tickets 101, 102 and 103), using the ticket tabs and the Cash tab as evidence. Each answer is in my words; the shaded boxes tie it to what happened in this app.

1. How would I evaluate the performance of the agents on each ticket, and why?

I think the agents did well. There is a little bit of duplication of effort, so I'd like to fine-tune that.

Evidence from this run
  • Ticket 101: right first call (Inventory) and the right result: two approvals I signed as Laila, invoice 501 for $840 (05:00:05 UTC) and the $8 purchase order for one tee in size S (05:00:11 UTC), plus an honest draft to Tauhid. But Inventory was asked three times (12 of 27 model calls, 71,688 tokens) and two more hand-offs were refused at the depth limit. That's the duplication.
  • Ticket 102: the leanest run. Facilities read lease 1, Accounting requested the rent, and I signed $2,400 rent to Elm City Properties (05:00:45 UTC). 12 model calls, 30,601 tokens.
  • Ticket 103: a good price decision ($50 per hoodie, above the $22 cost) and correctly no purchase order. No human approval was needed because no money moved. But the draft to Yale AI Club didn't quote the $50 or offer size L, and the Boss left the ticket in_progress, so I marked it resolved myself.
  • Money (Cash tab): every dollar that left the till went through one of my three approvals: $3,400.00 − $840.00 − $8.00 − $2,400.00 = $152.00, which matches cash_accounts. No agent paid anything on its own.

2. For each ticket, how did Actual compare to the Expected plan I wrote earlier?

I think it was good to work through it and process it, double check everything is working, and then fine-tune some of the issues I was finding.

Evidence from this run
  • Ticket 101: Expected 4 delegations with Inventory first. Actual: Inventory first, but 6 delegations + 2 refused, because Accounting and Customer Service each double-checked with Inventory. The end state matched exactly: 2 approvals ($840 + $8), a draft, waiting_approval.
  • Ticket 102: Expected Boss → Facilities → Accounting. Actual: Boss → Facilities, then Boss → Accounting, so Facilities didn't hand it off itself. The end state matched: one $2,400 approval, and lease 1 moved to 2026-10-02.
  • Ticket 103: Expected Inventory first and an Inventory → Accounting hand-off about invoice 501. Actual: Accounting first, and no hand-off, because I had already paid invoice 501 on ticket 101. Expected and actual both had no purchase order and $0 cash change.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

What I'm noticing is maybe there are too many checkpoints. Instead of using 6 delegations, we only need 4. So instead of having Accounting and Customer Service each double check with Inventory, just have Accounting check with Inventory.

Also, on ticket 102 the Boss sent Accounting the task itself instead of Facilities handing it off. I think instead of the Boss handing it off to Accounting, Facilities can do that, and that also helps with the operation. I think that would be helpful.

As one agent: for any sort of sizing or inventory questions, we actually could just have the Inventory agent handle that on its own as a single agent. Maybe there's an easy way for it to also just see what's in Facilities.

Evidence from this run
  • Ticket 101: Inventory answered the same stock question three times (check_stock, list_vendors and get_invoice each ×3). With only Accounting checking with Inventory, two of those rounds go away.
  • Inventory on its own: on ticket 101 and ticket 103, the sizing questions (0 tees in size S; 8 of 20 hoodies in size M) were all answered by Inventory's own tools (check_stock, list_vendors, get_invoice). The Boss, Accounting and Customer Service only passed those facts along.
  • Ticket 102: the whole job was two lookups and one request (get_lease, get_cash_balance, request_payment), yet it took 12 model calls across 3 agents.

4. Three new problems Campus Customs might face that this agent team could solve with the tools I built

I think this is going to be so great for Campus Customs:

  1. Quickly backfill any out-of-stock orders and keep our inventory up to date.
  2. Make sure we have our books in line, understanding what money is going out and what money is coming in.
  3. Give the Boss transparency into the operations, so they understand where they need to step in, step back, or fine-tune the operations engine, and make sure there's no duplication of effort.
How the tools already cover it
  • Backfill: check_stock, list_vendors and request_purchase_order did this for the size S tee on ticket 101, including waiting until the vendor's invoice was paid.
  • Books: get_cash_balance, list_open_invoices, list_payments and the Cash tab traced every dollar out, $3,400 → $152. Money coming in isn't tracked yet (no revenue in the database).
  • Transparency: the board's floor, transcript and run report, plus output/audit_trail.json, show each agent's steps, delegations and token use. That's how I spotted the extra Inventory checks on ticket 101.

5. New problems Campus Customs might face that this agent team could not solve with the tools I built, and what we'd need

  1. Customers asking questions in real time. When customers have questions, they need some sort of chat box or some place where they can talk to us. On the website we built a while back there is a chat box. I think that can be improved, and I wonder if there's a way to connect to it so that as customers ask in real time, they can also get the information from our Inventory, Facilities or Customer Service.
  2. Items that won't come back in stock. When we know something's not coming back, we need a way to flag that and make sure we communicate it to the customer. I'm sure the Customer Service agent would do that, but it needs to have the right information.
    What we'd need: a tool or agent that could see if there would be any reorders of an item, or if an item has been canceled or we know will not be in stock. I think a tool connected to Inventory would help.
  3. Questions about color or style. I'm assuming it would be the same as size, but I want to make sure we think that through. For example, if someone really wants a size small in blue instead of heather gray, could we say, "We don't have the size small in blue, but we do have the size small in heather gray. Would you want that instead?" That keeps the possibility of a sale going, which would be really great.
    What we'd need: that's definitely a tool. We should add tools that also let us know about colors, and see if there's a version of that for items that don't come in t-shirt sizes. And if we didn't have the item, or weren't going to get it back in stock, there could be a tool that shows similar items the customer may be interested in, based on their original choice, that they could purchase and that are in stock.
  4. Cash getting too low or an overdraft. How do we know when our cash is too low, and when do we flag it? When do we need to look at the finances, and what kind of message goes to the Boss or whoever needs to communicate it, so they can respond effectively and keep operations moving smoothly?
    What we'd need: a tool that flags when cash drops below $1,000, in case we need to re-up our account so we can pay our bills, and that immediately flags it to the Boss so they can act quickly.
Why today's tools can't do it
  • Chat: on ticket 101 Customer Service could only save draft 1 for Tauhid; the agents only run when a ticket is sent from the board, and Customer Service can only save drafts. There's no tool to read or answer live messages.
  • Won't restock: the database has no "discontinued" field, and no tool can mark an item that way.
  • Color and style: on ticket 103 Inventory could only check CC-HOOD-NAVY by size; the inventory table only has sku + size, with no color or style columns, so check_stock can't suggest "same size, other color".
  • Low cash: after tickets 101 and 102 the Cash tab shows only $152 left, less than the $264 it would cost to restock the 12 hoodies short on ticket 103. The tools refuse an overdraft, but nothing warns ahead of time or alerts the Boss. With a $1,000 flag, the Boss would have been alerted the moment I signed the $2,400 rent on ticket 102 ($2,552 → $152).

The plan assumed the tickets ran in order 101 → 102 → 103, each as its own team run, with no approvals decided between runs. In the Problem 9 run I signed each ticket's approvals before starting the next ticket, which is why ticket 103 found invoice 501 already paid.
